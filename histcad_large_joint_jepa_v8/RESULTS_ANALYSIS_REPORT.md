## Scope and reading rules

This is an analytical record, not manuscript Results prose. The principal evidence is the completed 12-representation benchmark in [`benchmarking/outputs`](benchmarking/outputs), read together with the original branch retrieval, retrieval-adapter, semantic-adapter, and STEP-only classification artifacts. All effects below are conditional on the saved datasets, splits, definitions, seeds, and checkpoint selection rules. Numbers from different cohorts or evaluators are deliberately kept separate.

The project directory is named `v8`, but its pretrained `outputs/full_dataset_4096/best.pt` is byte-identical (SHA-256 `fe54c5645b231887079bbe22d8b6e071d316f5355517f05f43cf35c8d348360d`) to the `v7` checkpoint. The two training summaries are identical. The TMCAD frozen-vector metadata and downstream configuration also explicitly point to the `v7` checkpoint and processed inputs. Thus the `v8` contribution in these artifacts is the expanded comparison and downstream analysis of the existing model, not a second independent pretraining replicate. The model is referred to below as **JEPA**.

The full HistCAD cache has 162,131 valid pairs (145,915 train, 8,130 validation, 8,086 test). The comprehensive benchmark retains 162,064 pairs and 8,082 test parts after matching BRepNet coverage. TMCAD has 9,312 prepared STEP parts (6,522/1,386/1,404); the BRepNet-matched benchmark retains 7,252 (5,085/1,077/1,090). The full-cohort final classifier uses all 1,404 test parts. The endpoint, trainable readout, and candidate bank must be stated whenever a result is quoted. [Sources: benchmark inventory](benchmarking/outputs/inventory.json), [pretraining summary](outputs/full_dataset_4096/training_summary.json), [TMCAD metadata](../histcad_large_joint_jepa_v7/downstream_classifier_tmcad_brep_views/data/processed/metadata.json).

## Main conclusions

1. **The clearest pretraining result concerns dimensional neighborhoods, not every notion of CAD similarity.** On the common 8,082-part HistCAD test bank, direct JEPA dimensional Recall@10 is 26.7%, compared with 20.6% for the same-encoder graph autoencoder, 20.4% for graph-statistics/PCA, 15.9% for the controlled contrastive model, 18.0% for the untrained encoder, and 10.2% for the externally pretrained BRepNet vector. Its standardized nearest-neighbor dimensional error is also lowest (0.265 versus 0.281 for the autoencoder and 0.289 for PCA). This ordering is consistent across two dimensional metrics. It is narrower than a claim of generally best CAD representation.
2. **JEPA preserves history and function information, but does not dominate those endpoints.** Autoencoder and JEPA have very similar direct operation-signature nearest-neighbor agreement (85.6% and 85.2%). The untrained same-architecture encoder reaches 96.0% for that metric and 99.2% for coarse feature count, while performing much worse on dimensional distance. PCA and raw graph statistics are also strong on the four-way final-operation probe. Canonical-function nearest-neighbor agreement is approximately 31–33% for JEPA, autoencoder, PCA, and the random control, so the direct joint vector has no clear semantic advantage.
3. **The joint vector is not yet shown to outperform its parametric branch.** On the original 8,086-part branch comparison, parametric-only and joint dimensional error are 0.268 and 0.265, and operation agreement is 86.2% and 84.5%; STEP-only is markedly worse (0.440 and 72.8%). The joint's dimensional advantage over the history branch is small, while its history agreement is lower. The evidence establishes that the history source is important for these HistCAD labels and that a joint vector is usable; it does not isolate positive synergy from fusion or prove that both branches are needed for the reported retrieval gain. [Branch evaluation](outputs/full_dataset_4096/retrieval_summary.csv).
4. **Geometry-targeted adaptation is powerful but changes what is preserved.** With the same 269,079-parameter adapter and matched HistCAD cohort, JEPA dimensional Recall@10 rises from 26.7% to 58.0% and error falls from 0.265 to 0.161. The adapted JEPA is ahead of adapted PCA (52.5%, 0.176), autoencoder (44.2%, 0.194), random encoder (31.1%, 0.257), and BRepNet (10.8%, 0.504). However, JEPA operation-signature agreement falls from 85.2% to 82.6%, and canonical-function agreement from 32.2% to 31.1% under this *controlled* adapter. The earlier JEPA-only 4.66M-parameter adapter instead raised operation agreement from 85.2% to 91.9%, while reducing primitive-profile agreement. Adapter architecture and objectives therefore materially affect the trade-off; these two adapter studies are not interchangeable.
5. **STEP-only transfer is encouraging but its strength depends on the readout and cohort.** On the shared 1,090-part TMCAD test subset, the fixed-capacity JEPA STEP adapter obtains macro F1 0.752, versus 0.549 for the autoencoder, 0.415 for the random encoder, 0.250 for contrastive, and 0.736 for BRepNet. The JEPA–BRepNet gap is only 0.016 F1 and has no reported paired uncertainty. Frozen linear-probe F1 is much lower (0.456 for JEPA), so the nonlinear downstream readout is doing considerable work. On the *different full* 1,404-part test set, the finalized frozen-JEPA classifier rises from 0.866 to 0.911 macro F1 when a 121-field physical residual is added (55 previously wrong predictions corrected, seven previously correct predictions worsened). That is a strong conditional benefit of extra physical measurements and supervised adaptation, not evidence that JEPA alone causes the full result.
6. **Text-derived semantic supervision adds function matching at a substantial dimensional cost.** In the matched benchmark, the same 329,088-parameter semantic adapter raises eligible canonical-function top-one matching for JEPA from 32.1% to 39.9% and top-five success from 58.2% to 67.0%, while dimensional Recall@10 falls from 26.7% to 14.8% and dimensional error rises from 0.265 to 0.372. Similar direction is seen for the autoencoder and PCA. The separate eight-condition JEPA study shows that explicit physical-size targets recover much more dimensional retrieval than sizes embedded in function text, with only small changes in canonical-function matching. No single trained vector is best across physical dimensions, operation history, and function labels.

## How the experiments connect

### Direct representation quality and the role of the two CAD sources

The original training objective combines masked joint/branch latent prediction, cross-source alignment, and a weighted dimensional loss. The validation-selected checkpoint is epoch 20 of a 20-epoch run. Its held-out dimension macro R² is 0.961, but this is **not an independent dimension-prediction test**: 16 standardized dimension-target components are inserted into the parametric global-node input. The R² verifies that the implemented pathway can recover that information; it cannot establish that dimensions were inferred from otherwise hidden geometry. Direct retrieval is therefore the more relevant evidence for whether the final vector organizes parts usefully. [Training summary](outputs/full_dataset_4096/training_summary.json), [input assignment](src/histcad_large_jepa/data.py), [loss weights](configs/full_dataset.yaml).

The direct dimensional ranking is quite stable against the main controls: JEPA beats the same-input autoencoder by 6.1 percentage points in Recall@10 and lowers normalized dimensional error by 0.016; it beats PCA by 6.3 points and lowers error by 0.024. The contrastive control is substantially weaker here, but its one tested NT-Xent recipe is a *controlled objective baseline*, not a reproduction of a named published CAD model. External BRepNet uses a different encoder and a segmentation-pretrained checkpoint, so the comparison is a useful transfer reference rather than an isolated objective test. [Frozen benchmark](benchmarking/outputs/frozen_representation/results.json), [baseline provenance](benchmarking/baselines/BASELINE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md).

The counterexample from the random encoder is informative. Its operation-signature, final-operation, and feature-count nearest-neighbor agreements are 96.0%, 97.7%, and 99.2%, all above JEPA, even though its dimensional error is 0.337. This indicates that these construction labels are highly accessible from the untrained graph input/architecture under cosine ranking. It does **not** establish that random features are better for physical similarity or out-of-domain use. It also means operation-label agreement alone is a weak demonstration of learned pretraining quality. The 4-class final-operation macro F1 is especially fragile: the `Intersect` class has only **two** test instances in the common 8,082-part bank. JEPA's nonlinear-probe macro F1 of 0.840 versus random's 0.934 is directionally unfavorable even when the three common classes are averaged separately (0.954 versus 0.979), but the four-class macro average should not be treated as a stable general-purpose ranking. [Frozen benchmark](benchmarking/outputs/frozen_representation/results.json).

The branch result explains why this pattern is plausible without overclaiming. Construction-history labels are derived from the parametric side; the STEP branch alone loses much of that signal. Fusion retains most of it, yet does not improve over history-only on operation agreement and improves dimensional error by only 0.003. Conversely, the STEP branch is the only branch available on TMCAD, so its transfer test answers a different question from HistCAD joint retrieval. A matched parametric-only versus joint comparison under the *same controlled adapters and all principal labels* is still needed to attribute an incremental benefit to the joint architecture.

### What the downstream geometric adapters add

The controlled adaptation matrix holds the projection width, trainable parameter count, targets, positive construction, and validation selection fixed across seven HistCAD representations. JEPA's final 58.0% dimensional Recall@10 is 5.5 points above PCA and 13.8 above the same-encoder autoencoder. Its error advantage (0.161 versus 0.176 and 0.194) agrees with Recall@10. This convergence across metrics supports a substantive, task-specific advantage of the JEPA initialization for numeric CAD retrieval on this cohort. It is not a label-free result: all adapters use standardized CAD geometry targets in training. [Controlled retrieval summary](benchmarking/outputs/retrieval_adaptation/summary.csv), [protocol](benchmarking/tasks/retrieval_adaptation/README.md).

The older JEPA-only adapter on the full 8,086-part test cohort improves dimensional Recall@10 from 27.9% to 53.0%, nearest-neighbor error from 0.254 to 0.169, and exact operation-history agreement from 85.2% to 91.9%. Its paired bootstrap interval for error change is −0.0883 to −0.0821, and 48.4% of queries receive a dimensionally closer top neighbor versus 5.9% a worse one (45.7% unchanged). It also lowers primitive-profile agreement by 6.8 points, with paired interval −7.6 to −6.2 points, and leaves canonical-function matching essentially unchanged. These are sizable, coherent changes, not minor fluctuations. The controlled adapter's different operation direction means the mechanism is **adapter-dependent**, rather than a universal property of JEPA. [Older adapter metrics](downstream_retrieval_adapter/outputs/comparison.csv), [paired diagnostics](downstream_retrieval_adapter/outputs/extended_evaluation.json).

The older adapter evaluator reports a direct JEPA dimensional Recall@10 of 27.9% and error 0.254, whereas the representation-baseline evaluator on the same nominal 8,086 test items reports 26.7% and 0.265, before the four BRepNet omissions. They use separate saved evaluation files/code paths. This difference is too large to explain confidently by the four excluded queries alone; its source (metric implementation, targets, vector processing, or artifact version) should be reconciled before placing the two direct rows in one figure or attributing the 53.0%-to-58.0% difference to a stronger adapter. The report uses each study's own control for within-study deltas. [Older comparison](downstream_retrieval_adapter/outputs/comparison.csv), [representation comparison](downstream_retrieval_adapter/outputs/benchmark_comparison.csv).

### STEP transfer, physical descriptors, and width

Within the **shared-coverage** TMCAD benchmark, JEPA has the strongest frozen nonlinear transfer F1 (0.752), but BRepNet is close (0.736) and has higher train-bank 5-NN F1 (0.703 versus 0.674). This is a useful separation: BRepNet's neighborhoods can be locally class-informative, while JEPA's frozen vector gives a slightly better result after the same supervised adapter. The same-encoder autoencoder, random, and contrastive conditions fall well behind JEPA after adaptation. The fully supervised from-scratch STEP encoder scores 0.761 macro F1 on the **full** 1,404-part test cohort, but it is an end-to-end 13.99M-trainable-parameter classifier with a different protocol; it is not a same-capacity head-to-head result against the 1,090-part frozen benchmark. [Matched TMCAD benchmark](benchmarking/outputs/tmcad_classification/summary.csv), [scratch control](benchmarking/outputs/baselines/tmcad_supervised_from_scratch/evaluation.json).

The finalized full-cohort TMCAD result tests a different contribution: adding 37 STEP physical descriptors and 84 surface-profile descriptors through a small residual to an **already supervised** classifier built on the frozen JEPA STEP encoder. Validation macro F1 rises 0.858→0.887; test macro F1 rises 0.866→0.911, accuracy 0.886→0.920, and balanced accuracy 0.860→0.909. The paired 55 corrections versus seven regressions account for the 48/1,404 = 3.42-point accuracy gain. Coupling recall rises 0.743→0.857 (70 test parts), and pulley 0.718→0.872 (78 parts). This pattern supports complementary explicit measurements, particularly on difficult classes. The physical residual itself is label-trained, so the result cannot identify a unique contribution of pretrained JEPA without a comparable non-JEPA base plus the same physical residual. [Final comparison](downstream_classifier_tmcad_final/outputs/default/test_comparison.csv), [per-class metrics](downstream_classifier_tmcad_final/outputs/default/test_per_class.csv), [paired evaluation](downstream_classifier_tmcad_final/outputs/default/evaluation.json).

The supervised-hidden-width ablation is **not** an ablation of the 4096-dimensional JEPA vector: it retrains 256- and 512-wide classifier heads and an identical physical residual. It selects 512 by validation macro F1 (0.875 versus 0.869 after the residual), but its selected test F1 is 0.879, well below the separate final residual run's 0.911. In the 512 arm, the physical residual fails the validation selection threshold and the reported residual row equals the base row. The valid conclusion is limited to a small validation advantage for 512 *within that fresh training protocol*; increasing supervised head width did not reproduce the strong physical-residual gain in this run. Given the previously inspected test set, this ablation is exploratory. [Width report](downstream_classifier_tmcad_final/width_ablation/outputs/default/width_comparison.csv), [width protocol](downstream_classifier_tmcad_final/width_ablation/README.md).

### Function matching versus dimensions

Direct canonical-function nearest-neighbor matching is weak and similar across several representations. The matched semantic adapters demonstrate that the text teacher can reorganize each representation: JEPA rises 32.1→39.9% top-one and 58.2→67.0% top-five eligible canonical-function matching, more than the autoencoder's 31.2→38.6% and PCA's 32.6→38.7%. Yet JEPA's dimensional Recall@10 falls 26.7→14.8%; the semantic endpoint and numeric endpoint move in opposing directions. The random control also improves only 32.7→34.1% in function matching while retaining more dimensional recall (17.9→16.8%), so semantic-adapter benefit is representation-dependent. These labels are dataset text descriptions, not independent functional or substitutability judgments. Eligible canonical-function queries form only about 46.7% of the original test split. [Matched semantic benchmark](benchmarking/outputs/semantic_retrieval/summary.csv), [eligibility audit](downstream_retrieval_adapter/outputs/extended_evaluation.json).

The eight-condition factorial study clarifies *where* dimensional information is retained during semantic adaptation. With size-stripped text, dimensional Recall@10 is 16.4%; adding a 15-component physical-size target raises it to 30.5%, while retaining numerical phrases in text alone raises it only to 18.5%. With full text and physical targets it reaches 31.9%. Function matching stays near 36.6–37.0% across these four conditions. The full 22-component target and extra count/operation heads do not materially improve dimensional recall beyond the physical-size condition; the extra heads do improve the outcomes they directly supervise. This supports an explicit numeric-target design for dimension-sensitive retrieval, not the assertion that a text embedding reliably represents engineering units. All eight adapters are six-epoch exploratory runs with validation losses still improving. [Factorial report](downstream_semantic_study/outputs_clean/FACTORIAL_ABLATION.md), [raw contrasts](downstream_semantic_study/outputs_clean/factorial_ablation.json).

## Claim assessment

| Intended claim | Current support | Appropriate boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Joint JEPA gives a useful CAD representation | **Supported for numeric HistCAD retrieval** on this split, with consistent direct and adapted advantages over AE, PCA, contrastive, random, and BRepNet. | Do not extend to all CAD similarity or function. |
| Predictive pretraining beats alternative objectives | **Partly supported:** JEPA beats the controlled same-encoder AE/contrastive conditions on dimensional retrieval and TMCAD adapted F1. | The objective bundles branch/joint losses, alignment, and dimensional supervision; a loss ablation is needed to assign credit to any component. |
| Both history and STEP contribute positively to joint retrieval | **Not established:** joint barely improves dimensional error over history-only and trails it on operation agreement; no controlled branch-by-joint adaptation matrix exists. | Describe both inputs and STEP-only transfer, but reserve a synergy claim. |
| Frozen JEPA transfers to STEP-only classification | **Supported conditionally:** JEPA leads AE/random/contrastive under the matched 1,090-part adapter; BRepNet is close. | State the BRepNet coverage restriction and the use of the `v7` STEP checkpoint. |
| Physical descriptors improve the final TMCAD classifier | **Supported within the final 1,404-part protocol:** 0.045 macro-F1 and 0.034 accuracy gain with favorable paired corrections. | The model is already label-trained; the additional 121 fields and residual are supervised. |
| Text supervision supplies functional CAD semantics | **Supported for dataset description matching, not engineering equivalence.** | Function labels are broad and coverage-limited; dimensional retrieval worsens without explicit numeric targets. |
| JEPA is efficient or few-shot | **Not established.** | Training is long, matched timing is incomplete across devices, and full labeled downstream splits were used. |

## Necessary next analyses, ordered by impact

1. **Reconcile provenance and evaluator versions before a combined paper table.** Record the checkpoint hash, vector source, dimension-target normalization, nearest-neighbor tie rule, and exact test UID list for the 27.9%/0.254 and 26.7%/0.265 direct controls. Recompute both through one locked evaluator if they are intended to share a row. This is a data-integrity step, not a new model experiment.
2. **Make a branch and loss contribution matrix on one cohort.** At minimum compare parametric-only, STEP-only, and joint vectors under the same direct and controlled adapted retrieval metrics. To claim a specific contribution from predictive loss, cross-source alignment, or dimension supervision, train removal ablations with unchanged data, capacity, and selection. The dimension-input overlap should be tested by withholding those 16 global-node target components in a separate run. These are genuinely necessary for architectural/component claims.
3. **Quantify uncertainty and robustness for close comparisons.** Save per-query/per-part predictions for the controlled benchmark and compute paired intervals for JEPA versus PCA on adapted dimensional recall, JEPA versus BRepNet on TMCAD macro F1, and the final classifier's gains. Repeat key pretrained and adapter conditions over independent seeds or use a newly locked test cohort. Do not present a 0.016 F1 gap as decisive without this.
4. **Audit the BRepNet selection effect.** Tabulate retained and omitted counts by split and class, then evaluate methods that cover all parts both on the 1,090-part shared subset and on their full 1,404-part test set under the same head. This cannot recover missing BRepNet predictions, but it reveals how much the common subset changes difficulty and class mix.
5. **For a function or engineering-retrieval claim, obtain independent relevance evidence.** Test function family or engineering substitutability with expert judgments and dimension tolerances. The current canonical labels and text-embedding overlap are informative proxies, not proof of interchangeable parts.

The first two items are needed before strong claims about the paper's **component contributions**; the last item is needed only if the intended contribution includes engineering relevance. Additional broad baseline collection is less urgent than resolving these specific gaps.

## Statistical-interpretation check

All 11 fallacy types in the experiment-validation checklist were considered. The material risks are **selection/survivorship and Berkson-style bias** from the 22.1% BRepNet TMCAD exclusions; **base-rate neglect and Simpson-type masking** from class imbalance and the two-example `Intersect` class; **look-elsewhere and forking paths** from multiple endpoint/model variants after earlier test inspection; and **causal overinterpretation** when assigning a combined model gain to one loss, modality, or pretraining step. Ecological inference is avoided by distinguishing per-part/per-query metrics from dataset-wide claims; collider adjustment and reverse causality are not central to these controlled model comparisons; regression to the mean is not an explanatory design here. No p-value or bootstrap interval from the controlled 12-representation benchmark was found, so close differences are described, not called statistically significant. The prior JEPA-only adapter's saved paired intervals are used only for that exact within-study comparison. Reproducibility status is **ANALYZED, not VERIFIED**: I checked saved artifacts and code/protocol relationships but did not rerun stochastic training.
